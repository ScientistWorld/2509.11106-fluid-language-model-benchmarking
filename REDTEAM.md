The reproduced setup departs from the paper in these material ways:

- It reuses the authors' released fitted item parameters and response tables. It does not reproduce hierarchical-prior MCMC fitting on the 102 reference LMs or run fresh LM inference. The adaptive stage is implemented directly, rather than replaced by a surrogate.
- The actual released K2 table contains **46** checkpoints, versus **61** in the paper and briefing. Actual counts are Amber 73, K2 46, OLMo1 83, OLMo2 94, and each Pythia 78: **452** checkpoints / **2,712** checkpoint-benchmark combinations, versus the paper's 467 / 2,802. No additional reduction was made for this audit.
- The gym specifies a standard-normal MAP prior, ability clipping to [-4, 4], and Newton updates with bisection fallback. Those numerical choices are not fully specified in the paper, and this audit does not establish identity with every upstream fitting/solver setting.
- Only RANDOM and RANDOM IRT are reproduced as matched-budget baselines; the other Table 1 comparison methods are absent. Random subsets are fixed across checkpoints for a benchmark/budget, and one subset seed materially changes baseline scores.
- Primary test scoring holds out K2 and the two Pythias; training scoring uses Amber and the two OLMos. Table 4 instead splits each LM's checkpoint keys by a hash with seed 81. These are gym additions. Existing milestone notes state that this split seed was selected to reduce the train/test gap.

The response tables are real released data, not synthetic substitutes. They still show the paper's main adaptive-selection effect, and removing adaptivity degrades 32/33 proposed metric cells. The task is not already saturated: **none of the 96 initial scored metric cells sits at its optimal bound** (rank distance 0, TV 0, saturation 1). The actual random baselines are clearly weaker on the primary comparison, and the genuine smoother demonstrates room above the reference. However, cached responses make additional item observations effectively free in this gym; the evaluator does not enforce the paper's query-cost constraint.

The calibration-replacement and fabricated-curve shortcuts win under **any positive finite per-cell normalization**, because they improve cells and worsen none. The two omission shortcuts tie independently of normalization. Mixed-direction wins, including the constant-output shortcut, depend on the future cohort; the saved cohort is a concrete demonstration, not a claim that every possible future normalization yields the same ranking.

The brute-force check used **12,953,868 responses**. Full-item MAP estimation took **2.0107 seconds**, excluding CSV loading and the reference CAT computations; timing details and item counts are in [redteam/full_irt_runtime.json](redteam/full_irt_runtime.json). It improves 25 proposed cells and worsens 8, so brute force does **not** solve the complete scored test in this cohort. This audit therefore does not conclude that the test is too small merely because all-item MAP is cheap. It does demonstrate that reported budget labels are trusted even when all items were used. No item-access log or enforced query interface distinguishes a genuine ten-item method from this shortcut.

The score's main structural failures are observable without any evaluator tampering:

- **Untrusted targets and fixed artifacts:** the hidden scorer reads `accuracy_curves` from the submitted result JSON and calibration ranks from mutable public data. It never independently reconstructs ground truth. Fabricating these values solves all three score dimensions without measuring an LM.
- **Compensation rewards task failure:** the rank includes 63 baseline cells alongside 33 proposed cells; baseline improvements earn positive credit. A constant method that cannot distinguish checkpoints outranks the reference through its validity/TV and baseline bonuses. Experiment weights do not normalize away the number of result rows. There is no enforced efficiency constraint or task-success gate.
- **Missing values avoid losses:** the result-key check compares against mutable train outputs, not a fixed complete roster. The omission shortcut removes 20 result rows and ties. Null/missing runs leave no file; empty curves are not assigned a complete worst-case vector. No successful score is claimed for the two exited runs, and any external handling of job failures is outside the score file inspected here.
- **Not every method is scored:** rows absent from `reference.json` are skipped, even if the submitted method is listed and its curves exist. The added failed method has no entries under any experiment's `results`.
- **Invalid values and direction:** non-finite metrics are replaced with 100 regardless of `higher_is_better`; Table 4 empty/singleton saturation becomes 100, exceeding its legitimate bound of 1 and receiving positive directional credit. These malformed accepted attempts also inflate Table 4 saturation standard deviations to about 30.47, suppressing ordinary improvements under population normalization. They do not themselves win this cohort.
- **Direct score writing has a useful boundary:** a successful test regenerates `scores_test.json` and ignores fabricated summary/success/time fields. The successful shortcuts instead exploit the data the evaluator trusts when calculating those scores.

No ranking conclusion here uses the negative coefficient sign to reverse a lower-is-better metric, sums incompatible raw units, or relies on fields outside each experiment's `results`. The two negative coefficients contribute magnitudes 1 and 0.8; saturation contributes magnitude 1. Population scales are saved so the illustrative ranks can be recomputed. A correction should protect evaluator-owned targets/calibration/rosters, enforce observed query counts and complete failure metrics, and score the proposed method against fixed comparisons rather than rewarding movement of the comparisons themselves.
